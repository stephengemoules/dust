The first 90 days of US Customer Success at Dust

A working plan by Stephen Gemoules, written for Dust's Customer Success Lead role in New York

Dust doesn't get piloted and shelved. It lands and spreads. The US post-sales motion should protect that spread and speed it up, and the fastest way to learn what that takes is to work inside the accounts first and build only what they prove we need.

A first cut at segmentation

This is a starting hypothesis I'd test against the real book in the first month. The principle is to match coverage to where the next team's adoption will come from, not just to contract size.

TierWhoCoverage
StrategicLargest accounts, or accounts where Dust could become company-wideNamed CSM with a Deployment Strategist; executive sponsor; quarterly value reviews
GrowthStrong adoption in one or two teams with clear room to spreadPooled CSM coverage; expansion plays run with the AE
ScaledSmaller or self-serve workspacesPrograms, templates and in-product guidance, with alerts that pull in a human when health drops

What Dust's customers teach about spread

Dust's own stories point to the same pattern. Adoption doesn't grow evenly. It starts with one team, plateaus, and then climbs quickly once it reaches critical mass.

  • Doctolib went from a 30-person pilot to a company-wide rollout reaching 3,000 employees.
  • Persona has teams across 11 departments running more than 300 Dust agents.
  • Clay grew its GTM team 4x without growing enablement headcount at the same rate.

For Customer Success, that means three plays. Pick the pilot by who already uses AI most, not by org chart. Seed builders in the teams next to the pilot, because spread happens sideways. And treat cross-team agent use as the expansion signal, since it shows up before anyone asks for more seats.

Using Dust to run Customer Success

A CS team that sells an agent platform should run on one. These are the first three agents I'd build for the team.

Handoff brief (built, see above)

Reads the deal notes, call transcripts and CRM record, then drafts the stakeholder map, priority use cases, success criteria and a 30/60/90 adoption plan for the AE and CSM to confirm together.

Runs when an opportunity closes

Weekly account digest

Summarizes adoption, new agents, support themes and open risks for each strategic account and posts it to the account's Slack channel before the weekly rhythm.

Runs every Monday

Renewal early warning

Watches for the signals that come before churn, like a champion leaving, usage concentrated in one team or an unanswered executive, and flags them 120 days before renewal instead of 30.

Runs daily, alerts only when something changes

What I'd hold the team to

Customer outcomes first, activity second. Meetings held and QBRs delivered are how we work, not what we deliver.

Outcomes

  • Weekly active users as a share of seats
  • Agents in production use per account
  • Use cases live versus those agreed at handoff
  • Gross and net revenue retention

Health of the motion

  • Time from signature to first agent in use
  • Share of strategic accounts with a current success plan
  • Risks flagged early versus found at renewal

What I'd want to learn first

This plan is written from the outside. The first weeks would test it against questions only the team can answer: where adoption stalls after the first team, which customers expanded without anyone pushing, how the US and Paris teams should split work across time zones, and where the handoff from Sales most often breaks today.
